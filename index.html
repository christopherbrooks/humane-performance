Humane Performance Initiative

There were several inspirations for this project:

  • ◦
    My love of classical chamber music.
  • ◦
    The intriguing example of the avant-garde movement of the late 1950s and early 1960s, the Beats, Abstract Expressionism, and musical experimenters like John Cage. At times, these pioneers forgot beauty (imho, a mistake), but their ideas remain fruitful.
  • ◦
    The incessant noise and high sound levels of the contemporary world (including popular music).
  • ◦
    The desire to break from rectangular screens and stages with a passive audience.
  • ◦
    Nature.

Principles

  • ◦
    All natural instruments (no electronics)
  • ◦
    Humane sound levels
  • ◦
    Intimate, quiet, spaces: audience and performers sharing the space (no stage)
  • ◦
    Serious art: beautiful, but never cloying or patronizing:
    • My compositions
    • In C, Terry Riley
    • Morton Feldman
    • Others? (Suggestions are welcome.)
  • ◦
    Low ticket prices, children free and encouraged
  • ◦
    Multiple performances per program
  • ◦
    Alcohol consumption encouraged (depending on performance location)

  • Jenny Huerterviola

    Jenny Huerter is an active performer and teacher of viola and violin in the Central PA area. She is Assistant Principal Viola of the York Symphony Orchestra and performs regularly with other groups including the Pennsylvania Philharmonic, Gettysburg Chamber Orchestra, Lancaster Symphony Orchestra, Fulton Theater Orchestra and many other orchestral and chamber groups in the area. She is Adjunct Professor of Viola and Violin at Lancaster Bible College and coaches viola sectionals for the York Youth Symphony Orchestra, as well as teaching privately from her home in Annville, PA. Jenny holds a B.M. in Viola Performance and an M.M. in Viola Performance and Pedagogy, both from the Peabody Institute of the Johns Hopkins University, where she studied with Victoria Chiang. In addition to her work as a musician and teacher, Jenny is mom to four amazing kids who keep life full and always an adventure.
